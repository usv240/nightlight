## 1. Local Alzheimer's Association chapter (find yours at alz.org/local_resources)

Subject: A dementia night-wandering tool that does not wake the caregiver: would you sanity-check it?

Hello,

I have built a free, open-source night-safety tool for families caring for someone with dementia, for the Amazon Developer Hackathon, and I would value five minutes of a practitioner's judgment far more than another week of engineering.

The idea: the Ring doorbell a family already owns learns the household's normal nights. When the front door opens at 3am, it first plays a message the family recorded, for example a daughter saying "Dad, it is night time, come back inside," and it wakes the caregiver only if activity continues. On 2,936 nights of real smart-home data, it removed 94.5 percent of the wakes a standard door alarm would cause.

You can see it in two minutes here, including a simulated month: https://d28hskpupjctiz.cloudfront.net

## 2. A dementia caregiver community (r/dementia, r/Alzheimers, agingcare.com forums; post, do not spam)

Title: I built a free tool so the door alarm stops waking you at night. Would you tell me what I got wrong?

Body:

My starting point was a fact from the caregiving literature: what usually ends care at home is not the disease, it is the caregiver's exhaustion, and every door alarm on the market wakes you for everything.

So I built Nightlight for a hackathon. It uses a Ring doorbell the household may already own, learns what nights normally look like, answers a 3am door opening with a recorded family voice first, and wakes you only if that does not settle it. Tested on 2,936 nights of real (research-corpus) homes, it cut wakes by 94.5 percent compared with a threshold alarm. It is free and open source, and nothing about it records video or audio inside the home.

I am not selling anything; this is a demo site with simulated data: https://d28hskpupjctiz.cloudfront.net

What I want from you is criticism from people who actually live this: what would make this unsafe, useless, or annoying in your house? And if anyone thinks it is right-headed, may I quote one sentence, anonymously if you prefer?
